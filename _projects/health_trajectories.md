**From Medical Charts to Market Forces: How Health Types Shape Labour-Market Outcomes**

MSc thesis, University of Essex, November 2024 (this version September 2026). Advisor: David Zentler-Munro.

[Read the thesis (PDF)](/assets/pdf/Qu_MSc_thesis.pdf){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}

Standard labour-supply models treat health as a static measure. Using 15 waves of the UK Household Longitudinal Study (UKHLS), I cluster frailty trajectories over ages 52–60 into latent health types with k-means, following Borella et al. (2024). I then test whether the types predict outcomes after age 60 beyond a static frailty measure.

- **Four health types emerge:** resilient (61%), mild (29%), frail (9%), and rapid decline (0.7%).
- **Future health:** the types reproduce some future-frailty results, to my knowledge for the first time outside the US Health and Retirement Study.
- **Labour-market outcomes:** the types add little for employment, pay, or hours once contemporaneous frailty is controlled for.
- **The exception is long-term sickness and disability,** where the types add predictive power that survives every static benchmark and a held-out test with the clustering refit.
- **Methods:** column-mean imputation on an unbalanced panel sorts respondents by interview coverage rather than health. I document this problem and correct for it.
