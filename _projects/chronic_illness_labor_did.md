Independent research extending my [MSc thesis on health types](/projects/health_trajectories/), 2026. Advisor: David Zentler-Munro.

Acute health shocks are sometimes fatal, and those who survive face an uncertain labour market. Employment effects that ignore this conflate survival with labour supply, and this project separates the two.

Using 15 waves of UKHLS panel data and a cohort aged 45–55 that is healthy at baseline, I estimate the labour-market cost of a first heart attack, stroke, or cancer. The design is a staggered difference-in-differences (Callaway–Sant'Anna) in which severity, a frailty index measured at onset, enters as a continuous dose.

- **Mortality:** the shock raises mortality by 6.2 percentage points.
- **Employment among survivors:** falls by 11.5 percentage points.
- **Total effect on being alive and employed:** −15.9 percentage points, so 28% of the cost flows through death.
- **Mechanism:** losses reflect labour-force withdrawal rather than job search.
- **Insurance:** benefits and pensions replace only a third of survivors' lost labour income.
- **Heterogeneity:** the cost is largest for the degree-educated.

Honest-DiD bounds and estimator cross-checks support the design.
